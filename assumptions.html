STIMULATED CATALYSIS / THERMAL BENCHMARK

a fair thermal benchmark

Equations, boundaries and the meaning of the break-even activation energy.

Compare the same rate

The measured enhancement f is stimulated rate divided by unstimulated rate at the same bulk temperature T₀, composition, concentrations and catalyst loading. Interpreting that ratio as a rate-constant ratio assumes comparable kinetic regimes. The thermal route must produce the same transformation. Equal rate does not imply equal yield, selectivity or mechanism.

The Arrhenius comparison

For a positive, temperature-independent apparent thermal activation energy Eₐ and unchanged prefactor:

ln f = (Eₐ/R) (1/T₀ − 1/T)
T = [1/T₀ − (R/Eₐ) ln f]⁻¹

All temperatures in these equations are in kelvin; R = 8.31446261815324 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. A nonpositive denominator means no finite positive-temperature solution. This is an extrapolation of the Arrhenius model, not evidence that a different thermal mechanism is impossible.

Heat capacity and energy

C = ρ V cₚ + Cᵥₑₛₛₑₗ
Eₜₕ = [C + UA (t + tᵣ/2)] ΔT / ηₕ

Density ρ is in g/mL, liquid volume V in mL, specific heat capacity cₚ in J/g/K and additional heat capacity in J/K. Operating time t and thermal ramp time tᵣ are in seconds. ηₕ is a fraction. UA is a constant heat-loss coefficient in W/K. The model assumes ambient equals T₀, a linear warm-up ramp, constant properties, no phase change, no heat recovery and negligible reaction enthalpy. Warm-up precedes the operating interval; reaction during warm-up is not credited.

The energy terms are sensible heating CΔT/ηₕ, warm-up loss UAΔTtᵣ/(2ηₕ), and holding loss UAΔTt/ηₕ. Holding input power is UAΔT/ηₕ. Total energy divided by operating time is an amortized average, not heater rating or steady holding power. Heater efficiency describes upstream conversion losses; do not include the same vessel losses in both efficiency and UA.

Find the activation-energy threshold

ΔTᴮᴱ = ηₕ Eₛₜᵢₘ / [C + UA (t + tᵣ/2)]
Eₐ,ᴮᴱ = R ln(f) T₀(T₀ + ΔTᴮᴱ) / ΔTᴮᴱ

Below the break-even activation energy, heating needs more energy to match the observed enhancement. Above it, heating needs less. This calculates a conditional threshold; it does not measure the actual activation energy or predict a stimulus response. With f = 1 there is no acceleration to reproduce and no unique threshold. With zero stimulus energy and f > 1, no finite break-even activation energy exists.

The maximum model temperature imposes a minimum feasible thermal activation energy, obtained by substituting ΔT = Tₘₐₓ − T₀ in the threshold equation. Below that minimum, this thermal benchmark is outside its valid temperature range and no winner is claimed. A mathematical break-even point above the temperature limit is labelled as extrapolated.

Energy accounting

Ideal mode: entered power is energy delivered at the chosen system boundary (incident optical, cell electrical, or mechanical/acoustic delivery). Efficiencies are unity and losses and auxiliary loads are omitted. Incident optical power is not absorbed power. Equipment mode: measured electrical power can be entered directly, or delivered power divided by an explicit electrical-to-delivered efficiency. No assumed conversion is hidden.

Eₛₜᵢₘ = (Pₒₙ d / ηₛ + Pₐᵤₓ)t + Eₛₜₐᵣₜ

For direct measured electrical power, ηₛ = 1. Duty d applies only to the main stimulus. Enter 100% for time-averaged power. Additional power and startup energy apply only in equipment mode. Include cooling or other non-common loads explicitly; shared baseline loads cancel. Both routes start at T₀. This is a batch-liquid comparison, not a continuous-flow or equal-conversion simulation.

Solvent data and limitations

Solvent heat capacities are selected measurements at 25 °C from the NIST Chemistry WebBook, converted from molar to mass-specific units. Density and phase-change values are room-temperature handbook approximations. The Sources page identifies each selected datum. Properties are held constant: any temperature change is an approximation, with an additional flag when either endpoint is over 20 K from 25 °C. That flag is a screening heuristic, not a validated accuracy interval.

Default temperature limits lie 1 °C inside rounded melting and boiling values at approximately atmospheric pressure. They are not kinetic validity limits. Reduce them to match experimental kinetics and chemical stability. Raising a limit past a preset boiling point does not validate pressurized-liquid operation. The tool withholds a preferred route there. Use a custom liquid only with appropriate properties and operating bounds.

Reading the plots

The activation-energy plot holds the measured enhancement and stimulus input constant while varying the assumed thermal activation energy. It does not infer enhancement from stimulus power. Curves stop outside the temperature limit. The time plot requires a known activation energy and assumes the entered enhancement persists throughout the interval. It compares startup plus operating energy; startup duration is not included in the horizontal operating-time axis.
